Knights of the Scroll

Whispers of clandestine operations, hidden meetings, and silent watchers naturally arise wherever civilization grows beyond the basics of survival. The paranoid suspect their opponents to be plotting against them, sleep-deprived guards swear they could hear footsteps and voices in the shadows, and the common folk blame hidden agents for odd occurrences and unexpected happenings. In many cases, these beliefs are nothing but the imagination running wild. However, on occasion, there is at least some truth to their suspicions.

Though there are many real culprits for this scattered across Aroka, the most often cited ones are the Knights of the Scroll. While their origins are unknown, some suspect them to be a surviving faction from the Age of Divinity that lives on into the Age of Reclamation to protect the secrets of the fallen world. Similar individuals and tactics have been reported in close proximity to the discovery of a new artifact, relic, or ruin with anything promising great power or wealth disappearing shortly afterward. This is not always the case, leading many to speculate that the Knights are not as omnipresent as some suspect, so those quick enough to act before the Knights arrive can maintain a hold of their prize. Ironically, this has often led to rash actions and conflicts that, after the dust has settled, seemed to serve as a diversion for the Knights to accomplish their mission.

On very rare occasions, a representative of the Knights will announce themselves to the local governors and scholars of a town or city, requesting access to their records or inquiring about strange occurrences. Such envoys are often well-guarded and in possession of enough wealth to purchase what they desire, suggesting that the Knights have the resources to act as they see fit, which raises more questions as to why they remain so secretive and from where they acquire their wealth. In many parts of Aroka, the Knights are nothing more than myth, but there are enough ambassadors that have made themselves known to local authorities to establish that they are powerful, resourceful, and widespread.
