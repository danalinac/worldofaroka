AOR 542-5-11

As soon as the artificial sun lit up, the party made their way down to the docks and boarded the Fortune Pursuit, one of the larger vessels of the Sea of Lights crewed exclusively by Cat-Kin. Due to Jack’s injuries from the past few days, he elected remain at Tyche’s home to recover along with Augustus and Helianth. The journey was uneventful. The party spent the time helping crew the vessel, watching out for potential threats, dredging for useful Flora or Fauna, and gambling. Ahgythah actually made a significant profit by gambling with the crew and using Tyche’s loaded dice. Fortunetly, Ahgythah was too intimidating for the crew to question her luck.

After a few hours, they arrived at the Temple of the Dying Light. The tiered cathedral sat atop a small island with many caves that had been repurposed into bunkhouses and storages room along with an extensive dock that had additional structures built on it. Once they were docked, they were appRoached by a representative of the Church of the Dying Light. As promised, Tyche was able to negotiate a better price for the Fortune Pursuit. True to their word, the Fortune Pursuit gave him 100 gold for his assistance. The catch was that the party would need to spend the rest of that day and half of the next offering their sweat to the Church.

The Church was responsible for keeping the artificial sun of the Sea of Lights shining. They kept it’s mechanisms secret, but it was common knowledge that it was powered by technological power cells charged at the Temple, shipped to the Tower of Light, then returned to the Temple when depleted. To charge the power cells, they had created machines that allowed individuals to bike, lift, pull, push, or run to generate power. Many had come to the Temple to pay tribute to The Light, spending days, months, even years toiling for the Church. In return, the Church would provide them food, shelter, and water for as long as they toiled. The party would pay their debt to the Church with a day’s worth of toil each.

Ahgythah, Chappel, and Perry did well toiling for the Church while Athyna, Devyn, and Tyche tried, but didn’t actually end up contributing much. Fortunetly, the Church only considered effort instead of results.

AOR 542-5-12

The following day, the party fulfilled their debt to the church. Considering their path forward, they asked the representatives of the Church when another vessel would come. He informed them that the next vessel, the Gnawtide Brothers, would arrive the following morning. Unfortunately, that was the same vessel that had cast him into the sea, so he really doubted he would make it back to the Isle of Lights alive. They would have to either sneak him aboard or somehow convince them not to kill him.

With nowhere to go, the party continued to toil for the Church to earn their food and shelter while they waited. Ahgythah offered her services as healer to the representative of the Church. To see if her healing abilities were greater than her physical strength, they asked her to attend to one of their devout toilers who repeatedly pushed himself beyond his limits and frequently passed out. They feared he would toil himself to death as he refused to rest more than was absolutely necessary and would not depart, no matter how much the Church pleaded with him. Taking them on on their challenge, Ahgythah appRoached the devout and through a combination of authoritative knowledge and intimidation, convinced him that he could better serve the Church and prolong the shining of The Light if he took better care of himself and gave himself time to recover. She provided him a break schedule, techniques to boost recovery, and some magical healing.

The representative from the Church took notice of Ahgythah’s skill and persuasiveness. That night, while everyone else was preparing for sleep, he appRoached her and asked that she accompany him with the understanding that she would tell no one of what she saw or did. She agreed and he led her through a doorway to a cave that led deeper into the island. Tyche, noticing Ahgythah’s depar, snuck after her, moving silently and sticking to the shadows. The representative led her through the maze of caves and tunnels to a large cavern. Beds, Tables, weapon racks lined the walls and a small, low-profile vessel sat on the water’s edge. On one of the table lay and Mouse-Kin barely clinging to life. An arrow protruded from his chest and a green ooze seeped out from the edges of the wound. A group of the Church’s healers stood nearby, clearly exhausted from trying to save the Mouse-Kin’s life.

Understanding the assignment, Ahgythah set About trying to save the Mouse-Kin through both magical and mundane means. It took all of her skill and much of her essence, but she was able to safely remove the arrow, cleans the venom, and stabilize her patient. By the time she got the Mouse-Kin to a point where he was more likely to survive than not, she was barely able to stand herself. Grateful, the healers of the church did what little they could to ensure Ahgythah would survive as well. The representative led her back to her party on the docks with Tyche still sneaking through the shadows and informed her that she no longer needed to toil in exchange for food and shelter and that their healers would attend to her in the morning once they had recovered their strength. With that, they left her to sleep and hope she had not exerted herself the point where she would not wake up.
