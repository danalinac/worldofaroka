AOR 542-5-71 to 76

After the battle in the pillar, the party spent six days recovering in Sanctum. During this time, they bonded with the Ahedyn girl they had rescued, naming her Athyna. How long she had been kept in captivity was unknown, but it had been long enough the she knew none of the modern languages of Aroka nor recognized any landmarks. Relying heavily on interpreting emotions and thoughts, she communicated with the party as best she could, using a mix of magical telepathy, hand motions, and sometimes interpretive dance.

After all of their wounds were recovered, the party said their farewells to the Last Hope and resumed their quest for Pallium. Serqet elected to remain behind with Ton, whom she had developed a bond with as they both enjoyed tinkering with abandon. To take her place, Ahgythah formerly renounced her role as the healer of the Last Hope, ready to venture forth into a new world. Additionally, Augustus the Otter-Kin, Je’Olt the Axolotl-Kin decided to leave the Crumbling Labyrinth behind and return to the surface with the party. Guided by the Hermit, they all crossed the bridge leading out of the Crumbling City, through a surprisingly intact passage through the mountain, and into fresh, open air.

A lake of fresh water had formed in a valley just outside the passage, allowing them the opportunity to wash of the dust and grim of the Crumbling Labyrinth before continuing on their way. As they crested a hill on the other side of the lake, they spotted wooden gates with figures atop them at the far end of another valley. Cautiously, they approach and could see that Dryn stood atop its wall.

Before they could reach it, a storm bore down on the valley, unleashing a downpour of water. Careful to not catch the Dryn by surprise and alarm them, the party lit torches and did their best to keep them from going out in the rain as they neared. As expected, they were instructed to halt, though the party was somewhat surprised that such an isolated Dryn population spoke the Common Tongue. Also as expected, they were told that they would be allowed entry if they surrendered their weapons. However, Helianth refused to part with his weapons, so the part was turned away and told to return in the morning. Annoyed, bitter, cold, and drenched, the party sought out a cave in which they could shelter and light a fire to dry themselves.

The following morning, the party returned to the gate and were greeted by the captain of the guard: Orik Fang. She agreed to meet with the members of the party that surrendered their weapons, which all but Helianth and Augustus agreed to. It didn’t take long for the party to realize that the Dryn were highly skeptical of non-Dryn, though it didn’t seem to be out of fear of the unknown. Orik explained that adventurers from the outside world would occasionally pass through their village, which she called Lex-Gate, teaching them the Common Tongue and trading for rare resources. However, since the start of the new year, the volume of adventurers had significantly increased. Initially, this had been a boon, but their most recent visitors had given them reason to be wary.

Not a month earlier, a company of Var’Yn came through, demanding the Dryn’s full cooperation or face annihilation. The Dryn of Lex-Gate were accustomed to trading goods and services with adventurers, but they did not have the numbers to defeat a company of Var’Yn. So, to avoid conflict, they handed over one of their meticulously hand-drawn maps of the Lex-Gate caverns that descended deeper into the Lost Mountains. While a steep price, they believe ridding themselves of the Var’Yn was worth it.

It took some convincing, but the party was able to assure Orik that they meant the Dryn no harm and would gladly offer goods and services in exchange for passage and a map forward. She agree to allow the party entry under the condition that they would always be accompanied by two guards per party member wherever they went while within their walls. The party would be allowed to retain their weapons, though they were encouraged to leave them at the bunk house they would be staying in.

Content with this arrangement, the party, accompanied by a platoon of guards, began a hike up from the gate to the plateau where the village was located. Halfway up, they came upon a pair of bunkhouses. One would house the guards assigned to watch them and the second would be theirs for the duration of their stay. As long as they either purchased, traded, or worked in service of the community and didn’t cause any disturbances, they would be allowed to stay and would be fed.

After depositing most of their gear at the bunkhouse, they asked the guards to tell them more about the community and show them around. There were five points of interest that they could explore. The first was the Lex-Gate for which the community was named; the second was the Gravestone, located atop a cliff looking out over the adjacent valley; the third was the Halo of Lex, the center of the community’s religious heart; the fourth was the entrance to the Lex-Gate Caverns the that the Var’Yn and all previously adventurers had disappeared into in search of Pallium; and the fifth was the village proper where most of the inhabitants lived. The remainder of the valley consisted of grassy hills for flocks and occasional fields for crops. There were also scattered clusters of trees that dotted the hills.

From here, the party split to cover explore the different points of interest. Augustus, Helianth, Je’Olt and Perry went to the village proper where they used their size and strength to help the Dryn build new structures in record time. While the Dryn were initially frightened of the Anthropes, their curiosity got the best of them and when they realized that the larger of the two could quickly erect scaffolding while the smaller ones scurried atop to fasten them together, they quickly set aside their distrust and embraced the utility of their guests. Perry especially made friends with the Dryn children by letting them ride around on his tail. They spent the rest of the day building with the Dryn and entertaining them.

Ahgythah, Athyna, and Jack went to the Halo of Lex. Ten tall, silvery metallic pillars rose from the ground in a circle with a ring of the same material sitting atop them. Stones had been stacked around and within it to form short walls and rough seating. Dryn in simple robes walked about, tending to the metal columns and the vegetation the grew around it. In the center sat a stone podium just large enough for a Dryn to sit atop with their legs folded beneath them.

Arch Vela, the High Priest of Lex, introduced himself to the newcomers. He and his head caretaker, Bren Aron, were far more accepting of the newcomers than Orik or any of the guards had been, conveying a sense of calm and clarity that seemed in starch contrast to a community surviving in the middle of one of the most dangerous mountain ranges on Sona. He explained that they did not fear the outside world as Lex would soon guide them through the Gate and back to their home. This response only raised more questions from the party, but Arch was hesitant to reveal much, stating that he did not wish to speak on behalf of Lex without express permission. He then offered for any of the party members that wished to speak with Lex to site atop the podium in the center of the Halo and see if Lex would reveal himself. He did warn that Lex was selected about when and to whom it spoke and that it had never spoken to a non-Dryn. Out of everyone in the party, he believed the Athyna may have the best chance as Ahedyn were often mistakenly considered to be a Dryn sub-ancestry.

Both Ahgythah and Athyna sat on the podium, but no images or words came to them. Arch was unsurprised and asked that the party return another time if they wished to try again. With that, Ahgythah left to see the Lex-Gate while Athyna and Jack went to see the Gravestone.

The Lex-Gate was a large square frame made of a mysterious material that pulsed with magical and technological energy dug out from the mountainside. Ahgythah recognized some of its parts from the wonders she had seen during the final days of the Age of Divinity, but she had never been an expert in such things nor would her memory serve her well after so many centuries. She noticed a small hut under a clump of nearby tried with an elderly Dryn man sitting in front of it muttering to himself.

Ahgythah approached and the man introduced himself as Kar Chron. He claimed to hear Lex’s voice and would catch glimpses of their home beyond the Gate. These claims had not been popular with the rest of his community, especially those that cared of the Halo of Lex, so he had decided to live next to the gate where he could meditate and dream of their home. Ahgythah was not able to get much more out of him, at least not anything that made sense to her.

Thankfully, a Dryn women with a large book bound on one hip and a pouch of tools on her other approach. She introduced herself as Cas Twyn, the chief architect of the Gate. While she did not show disdain or disrespect towards Kar, she did warn Ahgythah that she should not listen to his babbling unless she wanted to join him in his madness. While she could not bring herself to believe the man’s claims of speaking with Lex when even Arch was so rarely blessed with the opportunity, she did feel a kinship with him in his fervor for a way to open the gate and return home.

Cas explained that they believed that the right components and tools could be used to activate the Gate, opening a portal that would allow them to return home, wherever that was. The book she possessed contained everything her predecessors had learned about the Gate. She had been in her role for over a decade and had not been able to add anything to the book. If she passed before adding something to the book, much less something substantial, she would be the first in her line to do so and she feared that the people would abandon their faith that the gate would be opened and would disperse to find new lives elsewhere in the world. So, so spent nearly every day studying the book and the Gate, trying to glean anything new.

Ahgythah mentioned that she knew of two individuals from within the Crumbling Labyrinth that were adept at figuring out how things worked, especially when others had thought they had already discovered all of its possibilities. Cas was eager to meet these individuals and see if they could help her leave her mark on the book, but feared launching an expedition into the mountain would not be approved due to how few soldiers they had as well as many of their Seekers never returning after doing so. In recent years, exploration of the lands beneath the earth had been banned for their Seekers as they could not afford to lose more. However, she would bring this knowledge to the elders and see if something could be arranged.

Athyna and Jack made their way past the village proper, through a small forest, and found themselves standing before a stone obelisk sitting on the edge of a cliff looking out over the adjacent valley. Sitting on the cliff’s edge was a Dryn women. She didn’t seem to notice them, so they took a closer look at the obelisk. Many symbols in a font they didn’t recognize were carved into its surface with the older ones seeming to be at the bottom with newer ones being further up. The symbols stretched up higher than Jack could reach, suggesting that the Dryn either had to climb the obelisk or use some sort of ladder to be able to reach that high.

Before they could voice their confusion about the symbols, the women told them that they were the names of everyone in their community that had died along with a symbol that indicated how they had died. She stood and approached them, pointing to a symbol that looked like a plant sprouting from the soil. That meant that the person had given their life so another could life. She pointed to another that looked like a group of people clustered together. That mean that the person had passed away from old age, surrounded by loved ones. There were some with no symbol next to them, which indicated that the person had simply disappeared. There were a few other symbols, but the majority of them showed that natural causes was the greatest killer with sacrifice being the second most by a large margin.

The women introduced herself as Vera Sal, the historian of Lex Gate. It was her task to memorize her peoples’ history and recite it to all that would listen. Curious, Jack asked if she could tell them how the Dryn had arrived here. She told him that the earliest parts of their history are unknown as survival took priority over keep records, but they believed that they had lived beneath the mountains while the lands above were still frozen. When they thawed, they emerged from what were now called the Lex-Gate Caverns and found the Halo of Lex already standing. Their chief at the time was the first to hear Lex’s voice and the tribe settled in the valley, slowly building up defenses and tactics to survive the harsh world until they were able to live in relative safety. The rest of their history up until the present was rather uneventful with the exception of the discovery of the Lex-Gate when removing stones from the mountainside for construction. When they did, Lex had spoken to their High Priest at the time and told them that they would need to find a way to open the Gate and return to their true home. Since then, their community had been focused on learning whatever they could about the Gate, gathering whatever they could from passing adventurers in hopes that something would lead to a breakthrough the would lead them home. That had been centuries ago and the people were beginning to lose faith, even more so when it seemed that the adventurers that had passed through as of late had been more dangerous.

Unaware of how much time they had spent listening to Vera’s retelling, Athyna and Jack realized that the last of the Three Suns was beginning to set and that they should go round up their companions and return to their bunkhouse. Vera thanked them for listening and hoped that they would have time to sit down and tell her some stories of the world beyond the valley. She admitted that her mind was devoted to remembering her own peoples’ history, so she would be unlikely to remember much of what they told her, but she did enjoy hearing about way lay beyond her small forgotten pocket of the world.
