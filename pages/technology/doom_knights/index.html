Doom Knights

During the Age of War, many great and terrible weapons were forged to dominate their enemies. Among these were the Doom Knights, though some scholars believe that they may have originally been called the Doomed Knights. Crafted by fusing the body and spirit of a soldier to their armor and weapons, these warriors were sealed to a life of combat, using the technology to use their own spirits as well as the spirits of those they slew to power their abilities.

While incredibly effective, there was a crucial weakness: the armor and weapons continued to drain its energy reserve even when inactive, eventually consuming the Knight if a new source was not introduced. Inducing the Knights into temporary comas between conflicts slowed the process, but not enough to avoid having to sacrifice a magical object, a prisoner of war, or in dire situations, a member of their own community to prevent the Knights from deteriorating. This issue was especially prevalent when the Age of War came to an end and there was no longer a need for such weapons. However, instead of submit to their fate, many Knights chose to rebel, laying waste to all that opposed them.

Eventually, the Doom Knights were defeated; some at the hands of many powerful foes, but most were bested by simply outrunning them until the armor consumed them. Many of the husks were placed in museums as warnings of the horrors and sacrifices made during the Age of War while others were dissected for repurposing or study. The rest were lost to nature and time.

During the Age of Divinity, it was discovered that husks that were sufficiently preserved could be reactivated with sufficient energy. They lacked much of the speed and strength of the originals as the physical bodies trapped inside were nothing but ash and bone, but they could still defeat nearly any mundane opponent. They also lacked cognitive abilities with the minds of their occupants having long since decayed. However, simple commands could be instilled into them by encoding messages into their input energy. This made them excellent assassins, guards, and soldiers. However, these commands were also easy to overwrite and could be corrupted over time if not properly updated, leading to many horrific incidents before proper safety procedures were put in place. Towards the end of the Age of Divinity, advances in magic and technology made Doom Knights impractical and they became more of a novelty, were used for research, or were relegated to tasks to dangerous or demeaning for a sapient being to perform.

During the First Firefall and Age of Ice, most of the Doom Knights were lost with very few resurfacing in the Age of Reclamation. The few that did carried an air of mystery as no one knew where they came from or on who’s orders they acted. There were so few encounters with them that it was unclear how many remained active, though most could agree that there were at least two: the Sentinel and the Slayer; with the former seemingly patrolling a vast area of wilderness and defeating all that marred it and the latter seeking particularly dangerous or powerful beings and vanquishing them.
