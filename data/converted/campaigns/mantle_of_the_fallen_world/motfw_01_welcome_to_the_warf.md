##### AOR 542-4-40

Realizing that nearly all adventuring equipment was being sold at a premium, the party decided to earn some coin to cover the cost of the equipment they would need. Thus far, they had not needed cold weather gear, but the Lost Mountains were still blanketed in snow and the earthen Shallows were not known for their warmth. Additionally, armor, bandages, firewood, rations, and torches would need to be purchased in bulk just to cover the first leg of the journey. If possible, a pack animal, ideally one not adverse to mountainous and underground terrain, would increase what they could carry.

To accomplish this, they ventured into the forests to hunt, hoping to sell or trade their catches. They were successful, encountering leapers that were easy to kill and who's meat was somewhat of a delicacy. Additionally, they found a stash of expensive heirlooms and trinkets in their den. Returning to the Warf, they elected to keep their meat as rations and sell the heirlooms and trinkets. They were able to make a moDest amount of coin before one of the locals recognized some of the items, most of which had recently been reported as stolen. This brought the Warf Guard, who took the party into custody for questioning. They was successfully able to negotiate their freedom for the remainder of the treasure, but felt that their honor had been tarnished and committed themselves to catching the real thief.

That night, they set up camp not far from the leapers' den, hoping to catch the thief return to their stash. However, it quickly became apparent that there was something else in the forest that had taken an interest in them. Just beyond the reach of the firelight, they could barely make out a ghoulish face watching them. When they went to investigate, it would withdraw, staying just on the beyond the edge of their torchlight. Uncertain of what to do, they stayed at their camp, taking turns keep an eye on it while the others slept. When morning came, it was gone.

##### AOR 542-4-41

The following morning, they attempted to find traces of the thing that had stalked them without success. Intrigued by this, they began their trip back to the Warf to see what the locals knew. On their way, they happened to spot an Otter-Kin slinking through the tall grass towards the leapers' den. They lied in wait and ambushed him, finding a satchel full of trinkets similar to the ones they had recovered yesterday. It seemed they had caught their thief after all. Attempts to determine if he was working alone or in the employ of someone proved futile, so they turned him over to the guard and cleared their names, though they didn't receive even the dignity of an apology for being being falsely accused the previous day. With that business settled, they set out to see what they could learn About the thing in the night. Many few locals that were willing to take the time to speak with them shared as legend of a guardian of the forests and swamps they called the Warden. Encounters with it were rare and the stories Varied enough that most were suspect to feats of imagination or outright falsehood. 

Unsure of how to determine fact from fiction, they made their way to the main hall of the Warf. The building looked like a slightly miniature castle, as if whoever had managed its construction wanted the appearance of a regal and strong keep but was on a budget. Inside, the majority of the space was occupied with accountants and legal workers maintaining the shipping and trade legers. In one of the corners sat a collection of shelves that served as a small library. The corner of this library was roped off with an weathered guard sitting behind a small desk nearby.

Additionally, there were three individuals that did not seem to fit in with the regular denizens of the Warf. Two heavily-armored guards stood on either side of the roped-off area and a robed Owl-Kin stood within perusing the books. All wore an insignia of a shield with scroll contained within its bounds. While Perry and Serqet were unfamiliar with this symbol, Jack recognized it belong to the Knights of the Scroll, a rather secretive organization dedicated to the accumulation and protection of knowledge.

The library guard initially denied them entry to the roped-off Section, but the Owl-Kin requested that Jack be allowed to join him. He introduced himself as O'Ard, and Overseer of the Knights of the Scroll sent to monitor the activities of the Warf in response to the surge in travelers. He took a particular interest in Jack's decanter and offered to share what knowledge he knew About Jack's query in exchange for information on The Decanter. Jack was relucent to provide much detail, sharing only that that he recovered it from a ruin in the Sandsea. O'Ard was not satisfied with such little detail, but upheld his end of the bargain, telling Jack that the Warden is something ancient, potentially predating the First Firefall, and that it was likely not a threat unless provoked. He was unwilling to share more unless Jack did that same, so the conversation ended with O'Ard giving Jack a token with the crest of the Knights of the Scroll on it, explaining that it would grant him an audience with their order should he encounter them in the future. As they left the Warf and returned to the forest, they saw that many travelers had arrived throughout the day, requiring a town of tents and wagons to be set up outside the gate to accommodate them.

Reaching their camp site from the night before, they settled in for the night, ready to see what they could learn About the Warden. As anticipated, its skeletal face appeared just beyond The Light of their campfire shortly after sunset. They tried a few different tricks, including trying to surround it with torchlight and then closing in on it, getting as close as they could without carrying a torch, and meandering closer to it without taking too direct a path. In nearly every case, it was a few steps ahead, keeping just out of sight and reach. However, Serqet had a trick up her sleeve. She had been tinkering with scraps of metal and wire salvage from remnants of ancient Technology. With this, she was able to construct a small cart to help carry her equipment. Additionally, she had designed a light amplifier for it that could make even a candle illuminate an entire room or be used as a spotlight. Setting a fuse on it, she set it beyond The Light of the campfire and then corralled the Warden towards it by following it with a torch. When the cart's light flashed on, they had only a brief instant to see the Warden in its entirety as it moved with blinding speed to escape The Light. It was a large quadrupedal being seemingly made of something that looked like both metal and stone, through it's movement swift movement suggested neither. Two additional limbs protruded from its shoulders, tipped with what looked like long claws. It's face was more of a mask, concealing any expression of anger, frustration, or surprise. 

Before they could glimpse any more of it, it was once again in the shadows and seemed more alert and wary than before. Perry, emboldened by having at least glimpsed the thing, approached it directly and was rewarded with a quick slash from one of its arms. The attack didn't deal enough damage to threaten his life, but it was enough to allow Perry to reconsider antagonizing it further. Unsure of if they had pushed it too far and were now in danger, they remained close to their campfire, lit additional torches around their perimeter, and never took their eyes off the Warden before dawn approach and it slunk away into the darkness.

##### AOR 542-4-42

The following morning, they returned to the Warf, deciding that the mysteries of the Warden were not worth delaying their search for Pallium. The gathered what supplies they could from the forest and purchased the rest from the merchants, including a pack lizard to carry their extra equipment and provisions, and spent the remainder of the day resting in preparation for the long, uncomfortable, and dangerous journey ahead.
