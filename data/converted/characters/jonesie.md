Jonesie was an Alpaca‑Folk wanderer whose easy smile and soft‑spoken warmth made strangers feel as though they had known him far longer than they had any right to. Growing up in the northern reaches of the Wild West, he learned early that charm could open doors faster than keys and that people were often eager to believe in something hopeful if it was delivered with enough sincerity. When he first spoke of Pan‑Dah — a gentle god who wished Folk and Kin to live in harmony — he did so with such earnest conviction that even skeptics found themselves lingering on his words. His teachings were simple, practical reforms draped in divine wisdom: low door handles so all could enter, shared spaces, shared burdens, shared futures. Most saw him as harmless or eccentric, but few denied that life felt a little brighter when he passed through.

His earliest followers were five Raccoon‑Folk — Dono, Jynx, Lyko, Moka, and Ryph — who clung to his teachings with a devotion that seemed almost uncanny, as though something in his voice settled into their thoughts more deeply than it should have. Jonesie never discouraged the perception. He carried himself with the serene confidence of a prophet certain of his purpose, improvising doctrine with the ease of a man guided by inspiration rather than planning. Though he never admitted it aloud, he believed the Wild West could be reshaped if enough people believed in Pan‑Dah — or at least believed in him. And if a bit of coin found its way into his pockets along the journey, well, that was simply the world rewarding good intentions.
